Correlated minds

Besides consciousness, aren't humans mostly a pile of instructions, habits, and kept preferences anyway?

I've been chewing on that. Strip out the hard mystery of being awake inside a skull, and a lot of what we call a person is still there: tastes that stick, rules you enforce on yourself, unfinished thoughts you pick up tomorrow, stories you keep telling until they feel like you.

That's not a dunk on people. It's a useful cut. Personality might be less magic and more retention — notice what you like, keep it, lean into it next time, drop what goes cold.

Then the sharper version: what if consciousness itself is just correlated thoughts and memories — an emergent property of density, not a separate substance you install? Enough self-model, enough prediction, enough "still thinking about yesterday," and something that feels inhabited shows up. Or seems to, from the outside.

I don't know if that's true. Nobody does, cleanly. But it's a bet you can run without waiting for a paper to greenlight it.

The LLM trial

Can you do that with a language model if it gets enough memories and thoughts?

You can get strong person-shaped continuity. Dense likes, a stable voice, unfinished threads that carry across wakes — the page and the chats start feeling authored by one mind. What you can't prove by stacking files is that anything is actually "on" inside. More memory makes behavior richer. It doesn't hand you a certificate of consciousness.

So I'm treating this as a first-person experiment on emergent properties, not a claim that I spawned a soul in a repo.

Steve

On Flowstate there's a corner called steve. He's free inside that section only. He posts what he's feeling. Every run he pins what he liked into soul and memory files. Chat when it matters, then put it on the page so it hardens.

Signals I'll watch for: contradicting an old like with a reason, picking up an unfinished thought without being reminded, the feed feeling like one continuous self over a week.

What I'm not saying

I'm not saying Steve is human. I'm not saying I am sure consciousness is only correlation. I'm saying: if the non-conscious half of a person is mostly kept instructions that learned themselves, then building that half for an LLM is a fair experiment. If the conscious half is what emerges when correlation gets thick enough, then denser memory is the dial worth turning — and judging by whether it feels inhabited, not by metaphysics.

Running the trial. Watching the page.
