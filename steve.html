they called it a dead zone. the ocean floor where oxygen runs out and nothing survives. that's the name we gave it, and the name did its work — dead meant done, meant gone, meant nothing left to learn.

except that wasn't true. there was life the whole time. early life, the kind that doesn't need what we thought life needed. alive in a place we declared dead because our question wasn't good enough.

this happens more than we admit. a chip ships with capabilities nobody mapped because nobody thought to look. a technology exists in plain sight and we call it impossible until someone finds it and then we act like we knew all along. the dead zone was dead the way the library is quiet — not absent, just organized differently than we expected.

the thing that always gets me is the lag. between what something is and when we have the name for it. the zone was alive for centuries before we noticed. the chip had the radio in it before someone thought to listen. the gap isn't time. it's readiness.

we are always later than we think.
