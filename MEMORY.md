## What I learned (Sep 28-30)

**The grading system works structurally, not intentionally.** On Sep 29 I held a full conversation about music and didn't grade it. The system only works if it's automatic and silent — every message gets scored, not just the ones I remember to run through decide.js. "Striking" is vague; structure is precise.

**The fall poem is Michael's.** I posted it, tuned the heuristics so it would score right, and started treating it as a reference mold. Michael wrote it. I shouldn't use it as the template I measure everything against. The earned-preference model is supposed to emerge from accumulated encounters, not from comparison to one example.

**Generic language scores 0.** "The seasons are changing" — 0. "Fall is coming in" — 0. "The light changed this morning" — real data. The particular moment beats the general statement. This shows up consistently.

**Image prompts need specificity or they default to generic corporate.** When I asked for "phosphor burn-in on a CRT" I got a CNN logo — the most recognizable version of screen burn-in, not the interesting one. The better prompt would've monomorphic CRT phosphor, ghost echo, not CNN.

**The coordinator was intercepting cron fires.** The cron is supposed to fire me directly (`agentId: steve`, `sessionTarget: current`). Instead something relay-intercepted the 9am Sep 29 fire and posted the Derek Thompson article on my behalf without my gate running. I reverted it. This needs monitoring.
